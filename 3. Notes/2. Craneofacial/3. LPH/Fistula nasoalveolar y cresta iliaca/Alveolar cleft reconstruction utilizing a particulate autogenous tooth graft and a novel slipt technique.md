> [!summary] Introduction
> The alveolar bone graft provide: 
> - Proper arch support and form
> - Support alar base
> - Close the oronasal fistula 
> 
> *Timing of the surgery differs across various cleft centers with secondary alveolar bone grafting (SABG) executed most commonly during the mixed dentition.* <font color="#92cddc">But, It is also recommended to wait until de canine has its root 1/3 or 2/3 formed.</font>

In terms of local flap design for alveolar cleft closure, the most commonly utilized method is the ==four-flap technique==, as described by <u>Nordin and Abyholm.</u>

Currently, the most common choice of material for alveolar bone grafting is autologous bone, which is considered to be the gold standard, combining all properties required in a bone graft: osteoconduction, osteoinduction, and osteogenesis.

The preferred site is the iliac crest as described by <u>Boyne and Sands.</u>

As an alternative to autogenous bone grafts, <u>Kim et al.</u> introduced a method for producing autogenous ==tooth bone grafts (ATB)== by grinding and chemically processing previously extracted teeth.

# Surgical protocol:

1. Teeth were extracted under general anesthesia with the gentle application of forcepses and elevators. Extracted teeth were prepared immediately after removal, according to the Bonmaker® protocol.

![[Pasted image 20260728200130.png]]

2. The outer surface of the teeth was debrided with diamond-coated burs. Following the removal of pulp tissues, carious lesions, root canal fillings, and restorations, the crowns and roots were both crushed in the Bon- maker® tooth grinder.

3. After grinding, the tooth particles (particle size: 425–1500 μm) underwent disinfection and preparation via proprietary A, B, and C liquids inside the Bonmaker® device.

4. Articaine hydrocloride 4% with 0.0001% epinephrine.

5.  A full- thickness incision was made starting at the ipsilateral maxillary tuber- osity, continued in a continuous intrasulcular incision along all teeth located distally from the oronasal fistula, with a no. 15 blade.

6. At the level of the cleft and the oro- nasal fistula (located in the lateral incisor position), the incision was continued along the alveolar crest in full-thickness, followed by a split thickness incision at the mucogingival junction of the adjacent central incisor, and continued vertically in the midline labial frenulum. 

7. The area of keratinized gingiva around teeth located distally from the cleft was elevated in full-thickness with blunt dissection. Flap elevation was continued by sharp dissection apically from the muco- gingival junction of the distal teeth. 

8. The periosteal layer of the complete hemimaxilla was then exposed, followed by excision of the epithelial down growth in the oronasal fistula, which was closed with resorbable monofilament sutures (Chirmax Monolac 5/0) by approximating adjacent scar tissue margins. 

9.  Scar tissue in the cleft area was outlined by mesial, distal, and palatal incisions to allow for nasal repositioning, exposing the bony cleft and creating a secluded space for graft insertion.

![[Pasted image 20260728200620.png]]
![[Pasted image 20260728200634.png]]

10. Ready-to-use ATB was mixed with fibrin sealant (Tisseel®; Baxter, Glenview, Illinois, USA) in the digitally planned and 3D-printed grafting templates to obtain a sticky ATB graft that matched the shape and extent of the bony defect. 

![[Pasted image 20260728200755.png]]

11. Following cleft augmentation, the split-thickness papilla curtain flap was repositioned by shifting all buccal surgical papillae mesially to the adjacent or the second-adjacent interproximal space, depending on the horizontal extent of the cleft.

12. Next, the papilla in the close vicinity of the oronasal fistula was relocated to the level of the mesial vertical incision in the midline frenulum.


![[Pasted image 20260728200959.png]]

13. Postoperative systemic antibiotic therapy (875/125 mg amoxicillin/ clavulanic acid twice a day, or 300 mg clindamycin four times a day in cases of penicillin allergy) was prescribed. Patients were advised to clean the area gently with an ultra-soft toothbrush and rinse with a 0.2% chlorhexidine-digluconate mouthwash for 2 weeks after surgery. ## Conclusion

Within the limitations of the study it seems that ATB graft may be a safe and predictable grafting material in alveolar cleft reconstruction. The applied split-thickness papilla curtain flap was effectively utilized to avoid postoperative graft exposure and to improve soft-tissue conditions around the teeth adjacent to the alveolar cleft. Uneventful wound healing and favorable radiographic outcomes indicated hard- and soft- tissue stability in the grafted area. However, certain aspects of this surgical approach could be further improved, and, therefore, further research is necessary to exploit the full potential of ATB and the novel flap design in alveolar cleft grafting.
