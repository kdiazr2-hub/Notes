# Mechanisms of embryology and early embryonic development

**In one sentence:** development is a **timetable**, so a single insult produces a different syndrome depending on **the day it hits** and on **which cell population** it hits — and the neural crest, which builds face, thymus and great vessels, is why one teratogen can damage four organs at once.

**Index:** [[Embryology and Craniofacial Growth - Index]] · **Next:** [[02. Early orofacial development and the neural crest]]

|---|---|---|

| **Notochord** | From the cranial end of the streak | **Induces** the overlying ectoderm → neural plate |

## Essentials

1. **The primitive streak makes the mesoderm**, because mesenchyme proliferates from it and migrates between the two original layers. The notochord is *also* mesoderm (cranial end of the streak), so it is never one of the "mesoderm-free" sites.
2. **Two sites stay bilaminar: oropharyngeal and cloacal membranes**, because the mesoderm cannot get between ectoderm and endoderm there. The oropharyngeal membrane is the future mouth–pharynx boundary and ruptures at day 28 to open the gut.
3. **The neural crest is a fourth germ layer in practice**: pluripotential, highly migratory, and the source of facial bone, cartilage, dentine, thymic stroma, aortic arch artery walls and melanocytes (Table 2-2). Damage to it gives a **neurocristopathy** — several organs at once, not an isolated defect.
4. **Crest gives connective tissue, mesoderm gives muscle.** The crest supplies the bone, cartilage and the connective-tissue covering of the arch muscles, but the muscles themselves come from paraxial mesoderm (somitomeres).
5. **The nerve follows the arch.** So nerve + muscle group always travel together: V with mastication (arch 1), VII with facial expression (arch 2). Knowing the nerve lets you deduce the arch.
6. **Apoptosis is constructive**, because tissues that are useful only in embryonic life must be removed on schedule. If the **nasal fin** fails to disintegrate (d47–48), the upper lip never achieves continuity → **cleft lip**. State it as: *if tissue X does not die, then Y does not open / does not fuse.*
7. **Hard tissues can only grow by apposition**, because a mineralized matrix cannot expand from within. Soft tissue and cartilage grow interstitially — which is why the cranial growth centres (synchondroses, condyle) are cartilaginous, and why facial bones (unlike long bones) keep appositional potential into adult life (acromegaly).
8. **The critical period of a structure is the peak of its own morphogenesis.** The embryonic period (weeks 1–8) is not one window but a sequence of windows.

## Don't confuse

| A | B | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| **Menstrual age** (from last period) | **Fertilization age** (from conception) | Sperber uses fertilization age. **Subtract 2 weeks** from what the patient says |
| Primitive streak → mesenchyme → mesoderm | Mesenchyme → streak | The streak comes first; mesenchyme is what it produces |
| Notochord (is mesoderm, induces the neural plate) | Oropharyngeal / cloacal membranes (no mesoderm) | The "mesoderm-free" sites are the two **poles** of the disk, not the midline |
| **Arch 1** = V + mastication + malleus/incus | **Arch 2** = VII + facial expression + stapes | Nerve and muscles always belong to the same arch |
| **Malformation** (genetic, embryonic period, not self-correcting) | **Deformation / disruption** (environmental, mechanical, vascular; fetal period, may self-correct) | Ask *when*: embryonic → malformation; fetal → deformation |
| **Window of exposure** | **Day the drug was stopped** | The damage happens when the target structure is being built, not when the mother finds out |
| Treacher Collins (mainly genetic, *TCOF1*) | Retinoic acid embryopathy (teratogenic **phenocopy**) | Same phenotype, different cause; the teratogen imitates it depending on timing |

## Case

A 24-year-old woman takes **isotretinoin** for acne. She discovers she is pregnant at **8 weeks by her last menstrual period** and stops the drug that day. The baby is born with a hypoplastic **right mandible** and **microtia** on the same side, **thymic hypoplasia** and a **conotruncal cardiac defect**.

> [!example]- Resolution
> - **Convert the clock.** 8 menstrual weeks − 2 = 6 fertilization weeks = **day 42**. But she had been taking the drug for acne all along, so the **window of exposure runs from day 0 to day 42**.
> - **Locate the damage, not the stop date.** Within that window, the vulnerable moment for these organs is **days 21–28**, when crest cells migrate and arches 1–4 form. Day 42 is only when exposure ended.
> - **One cell population explains four organs.** Isotretinoin destroys neural crest cells and disrupts their migration. Crest from r1–r2 and r4 → mandible and ear (arches 1–2); crest from r6–r7 → arches 3, 4 and 6 → **thymic stroma** and **aortic arch artery walls** (Table 2-2). Hence mandible + ear + thymus + outflow tract.
> - **Name it:** a **neurocristopathy** — here a retinoic acid embryopathy producing a phenocopy of craniofacial microsomia. CFM itself is multifactorial (vascular disruption of the stapedial artery, teratogens, genetic forms), and Treacher Collins is mainly *TCOF1*.
> - **Counterfactual.** Had the exposure been limited to **day 35–45**, arches would already be built and the expected defect would be **cleft lip/palate** (lip d37–41, palate d44–60) instead.

## Test yourself

> [!question]- Which structure generates the intraembryonic mesoderm, and which two sites never receive it?
> The **primitive streak**. The **oropharyngeal** and **cloacal membranes** remain bilaminar.

> [!question]- Is the notochord mesoderm or not? What does it induce?
> It **is** mesoderm (from the cranial end of the primitive streak). It induces the overlying ectoderm to form the **neural plate** → neural tube → CNS.

> [!question]- Where exactly does the neural crest arise, and why is it called a fourth germ layer?
> At the **crest of the neural folds**, where neuralizing and epidermalizing influences meet. It is pluripotential and migratory, like a germ layer.

> [!question]- Which rhombomeres are crest-free?
> **r3 and r5.**

> [!question]- Arch 1 vs arch 2: nerve, muscles, ossicle
> Arch 1: trigeminal (V), muscles of mastication, malleus and incus. Arch 2: facial (VII), muscles of facial expression, stapes.

> [!question]- The crest builds the facial skeleton. Where do the muscles of the arches come from?
> **Paraxial mesoderm (somitomeres).** The crest only contributes their connective-tissue covering.

> [!question]- Name three non-craniofacial derivatives of the neural crest that matter clinically
> Thymic stroma, walls of the aortic arch arteries, melanocytes. (Also adrenal medulla, parathyroid stroma, enteric ganglia.)

> [!question]- What happens if the nasal fin fails to disintegrate, and by what mechanism should it disappear?
> **Cleft lip**: the upper lip never achieves continuity. The mechanism is **apoptosis** (d47–48).

> [!question]- Why can bone and teeth not grow interstitially?
> A mineralized matrix cannot expand from within, so hard tissues grow only by **apposition**. Cartilage and soft tissue grow interstitially.

> [!question]- A patient says she is 9 weeks pregnant. What fertilization day is that, and what is being built?
> 9 − 2 = 7 weeks = **day 49**. The **palate** (shelves elevate d56, fuse d60).

> [!question]- Malformation vs deformation: timing and reversibility
> Malformation: genetic, **embryonic** period, does not self-correct. Deformation/disruption: environmental, mechanical or vascular, **fetal** period, may self-correct.

> [!question] Which rhombomeres feed arch I, and which send nothing at all?
> ![[Sperber Fig 2-14 - Neural crest migration from rhombomeres to pharyngeal arches.jpg|520]]
> > [!success]- Answer
> > **r1 and r2** → arch I. **r3 and r5** are crest-free. r4 → arch II; r6–r7 → arches III, IV and VI.

> [!question] In this section, what are the two structures the mesoderm never separates?
> ![[Sperber Fig 2-10 - Embryonic disk with prechordal plate and primitive streak.jpg|460]]
> > [!success]- Answer
> > The **oropharyngeal membrane** (at the prechordal plate, cranially) and the **cloacal membrane** (caudally). Both stay bilaminar, ectoderm against endoderm.

> [!question] Exposure to a teratogen on day 25 post conception: what malformation would you expect, and why?
> ![[Sperber Table 2-1 - Chronology of the embryonic period.jpg|520]]
> > [!success]- Answer
> > Crest migration (d21) and arch formation (d24–28) are under way → a **craniofacial microsomia-like** defect of arches 1 and 2. The lip (d37–41) and palate (d44–60) have not started yet.

## My mistakes

- I said the mesenchyme gives rise to the mesoderm, and that the **notochord** was the site the mesoderm does not reach. Both are backwards: the **primitive streak** makes the mesenchyme that *is* the mesoderm, and the notochord is itself mesoderm. The mesoderm-free sites are the oropharyngeal and cloacal **membranes**.
- I put the **facial nerve and the muscles of facial expression in arch 1**, while correctly excluding the stapes from it. I had the ossicle rule but not the rule that **each arch carries its own nerve and its own muscles**. I also forgot the mandible, which is the main arch-1 structure.
- Asked why a failure of apoptosis causes a malformation, I answered vaguely ("it affects other structures") and invoked **teratomas**, which is a different entity. The concrete statement is: the tissue that should die persists, so a passage does not open or a fusion does not occur.
- I treated the **embryonic period as a single window**, so I judged a day-35 exposure as still within the crest-migration period. Each structure has **its own day**; arches are already built by day 28.
- I mixed the **two clocks**: I compared the patient's menstrual weeks with Sperber's fertilization-age table. Always subtract 2 weeks first. In one calculation I also converted 4 weeks into 16 days instead of 28.
- In the case I said the damage happened at day 42, the day the drug was **stopped**. The damage happens when the **target structure is being built** (d21–28); the stop date only closes the exposure window.
