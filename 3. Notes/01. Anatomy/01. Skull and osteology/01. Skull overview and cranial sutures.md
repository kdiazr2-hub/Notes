# The skull: bones, sutures and fontanelles

**In one sentence:** the skull is 22 bones joined by sutures that act as **growth sites**. Knowing where they meet and when they close explains craniosynostosis, the shape of an epidural hematoma and zygomatic fractures.

**Index:** [[Anatomy - Index]] · **Next:** [[02. Individual skull bones and cervical vertebrae]]

|---|---|---|
| **Neurocranium (8)** | Frontal → ethmoid → sphenoid → occipital (unpaired) · temporals and parietals (paired) | 4 in the midline from front to back + 2 pairs on the sides |

**Calvarial sutures**

| Suture | Joins | Direction → growth it allows |
|---|---|---|
| Metopic | The 2 halves of the frontal bone | Anteroposterior (midline) → widens the forehead |
| Coronal | Frontal – parietals | Transverse → lengthens the skull |
| Sagittal | Parietal – parietal | Anteroposterior (midline) → widens the skull |
| Lambdoid | Parietals – occipital | Transverse (λ-shaped) → lengthens the skull posteriorly |
| Squamous | Temporal – parietal | Lateral |

**Meeting points and fontanelles**

| Point | What meets | Fontanelle (newborn) | Closure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bregma** | Coronal + sagittal | Anterior: **diamond** (4 bones) | ~18 months (highly variable) |
| **Lambda** | Sagittal + lambdoid | Posterior: **triangle** (3 bones) | ~3 months |
| **Pterion** | Frontal + parietal + **greater wing of the sphenoid** + temporal ("H") | Anterolateral (sphenoidal) | — |
| **Asterion** | Lambdoid + parietomastoid + occipitomastoid → parietal, occipital, temporal (mastoid part) | Posterolateral (mastoid) | — |

```
      FRONTAL      │   PARIETAL
  ─────────────────┼─────────────────   ← pterion (lateral view)
     SPHENOID      │   TEMPORAL
   (greater wing)  │   (squama)
```

## Essentials

1. **The temporal bone never reaches the vertex.** Its neighbors are the parietal above (squamous suture), the occipital behind (occipitomastoid suture), the greater wing of the sphenoid in front, and the zygoma through the arch. That is why the coronal suture ends at the pterion and the frontal does not touch the temporal: the sphenoid is in between.
2. **At birth, the frontal bone is two bones.** The metopic suture is the first to close physiologically: on CT, all are closed by 9 months (Vu 2001). A closed metopic suture in an infant is not, by itself, pathological.
3. **Virchow's law.** When a suture fuses prematurely, growth **perpendicular** to it is lost and the skull compensates by growing **parallel** to it. First identify which way the suture runs, then deduce the shape.
   - Sagittal → long, narrow skull = **scaphocephaly** (the most common).
   - Metopic → pointed forehead and wide parietal region = **trigonocephaly**, with **hypotelorism**.

   ![[Thaller Fig 1 - Scaphocephaly from sagittal synostosis.jpg|380]] ![[Di Rocco Fig 44.2 - Trigonocephaly (superior view).jpg|260]]
   *Scaphocephaly: long, narrow skull (Thaller, Fig. 1). Trigonocephaly: keel-shaped forehead and widened parieto-occipital region (Di Rocco, Fig. 44.2).*
4. **Pterion = the thinnest part of the lateral wall**, over the anterior branch of the **middle meningeal artery** → epidural hematoma.
5. **An epidural hematoma does not cross sutures**, because the dura is most firmly attached to bone there. That is why it is **biconvex**. A subdural hematoma does cross sutures and is crescent-shaped.
6. **The zygoma articulates with 4 bones:** frontal, maxilla, temporal (at the arch) and sphenoid (in the lateral orbital wall). The **sphenozygomatic suture** is the best landmark to confirm the zygoma has been correctly reduced (Perry & Holmes, p. 149).

   ![[Iwanaga Fig 2.22 - Sutures of the lateral orbital wall.jpg|560]]
   *Iwanaga, Fig. 2.22: the sphenozygomatic suture runs inside the orbit, behind the rim.*
7. **The anterior fontanelle is a window to the infant's brain:** bulging = raised intracranial pressure; sunken = dehydration.

## Don't confuse

| A | B | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Pterygopalatine fossa | Suture | The fossa is a **space** behind the maxilla, not a joint between bones |
| Mastoid | Bone | It is a **part of the temporal bone** |
| Inferior nasal concha | Superior and middle conchae | The inferior concha is a **separate bone**; the superior and middle ones are parts of the **ethmoid** |
| Squamous suture (temporal–parietal) | Occipitomastoid suture (temporal–occipital) | The squamous suture is above the ear; the occipitomastoid is behind it |
| Epidural hematoma (biconvex, does not cross sutures, arterial) | Subdural hematoma (crescent, crosses sutures, venous) | Check whether the collection stops at a suture |
| Ethmoid (anterior, between the orbits) | Sphenoid (posterior, sella turcica) | Order of the unpaired bones: **F-E-S-O** |

## Case

A 24-year-old man is hit on the left temple with a baseball bat. He loses consciousness for a few seconds, recovers and talks normally. Three hours later he is drowsy, with a severe headache and a **dilated left pupil**.

> [!example]- Resolution
> - **Fracture at the pterion**: it is the thinnest part of the lateral wall.
> - **Bleeding from the anterior branch of the middle meningeal artery.** Blood collects between the bone and the dura: an **epidural hematoma**.
> - **Lucid interval.** The bleeding is arterial, but blood takes time to strip the adherent dura and the brain compensates at first. Once compensation is exhausted, the patient deteriorates abruptly.
> - **CT:** a **biconvex** collection that stops at the sutures.
> - **Ipsilateral dilated pupil:** the hematoma pushes the uncus of the temporal lobe through the tentorial notch (**uncal herniation**), and the uncus compresses **CN III** against the tentorial edge. The parasympathetic fibers that constrict the pupil run **on the surface** of the nerve, so mydriasis appears before paralysis of the eye muscles. It is on the same side because the mass is on that side.
> - It is a **surgical emergency**: craniotomy and evacuation of the hematoma.

## Test yourself

> [!question]- Which 4 bones form the pterion?
> Frontal, parietal, greater wing of the sphenoid and temporal (H-shaped).

> [!question]- Which bones does the squamous suture join? And the metopic suture?
> Squamous: temporal–parietal. Metopic: the two halves of the frontal bone.

> [!question]- Where does the coronal suture end, and why doesn't the frontal bone touch the temporal?
> At the pterion: the greater wing of the sphenoid is in between.

> [!question]- Anterior fontanelle: where is it, what shape is it and when does it close?
> At the bregma. It is diamond-shaped (4 bones) and closes at about 18 months, with wide variability.

> [!question]- Sagittal synostosis: what shape does the skull take, and why?
> Scaphocephaly (long and narrow). Transverse growth, perpendicular to the suture, is lost, and the skull compensates by growing front to back.

> [!question]- Metopic synostosis: skull shape and orbital sign
> Trigonocephaly with hypotelorism: the forehead cannot widen.

> [!question]- Which bones meet at the asterion?
> Parietal, occipital and temporal (mastoid part).

> [!question]- Why is an epidural hematoma biconvex?
> The dura is most firmly attached to bone at the sutures, so the hematoma cannot cross them and builds up inward.

> [!question] Point to the pterion and name the 4 bones that form it
> ![[Iwanaga Fig 2.6 - Lateral view of the skull.jpg|420]]
> > [!success]- Answer
> > It lies in the temple, just above and behind the frontal process of the zygoma. It is formed by the frontal, parietal, greater wing of the sphenoid and temporal bones.

> [!question] Which craniosynostosis is this, and which suture fused?
> ![[Di Rocco Fig 44.2 - Trigonocephaly (superior view).jpg|220]]
> > [!success]- Answer
> > Trigonocephaly from metopic synostosis: keel-shaped forehead and widened parieto-occipital region. Also look for hypotelorism.

> [!question]- Which 4 bones does the zygoma articulate with, and which suture confirms reduction?
> Frontal, maxilla, temporal and sphenoid. The sphenozygomatic suture confirms reduction.

## My mistakes

- I put **3 bones at the pterion**: I left out the greater wing of the sphenoid.
- I placed the **temporal bone too high**: I included it in the coronal suture (frontal–parietals only), joined it to the occipital in the squamous suture (it is the parietal) and put it in the metopic suture (the two halves of the frontal).
- I reasoned **trigonocephaly** as "it grows up and down". The metopic suture runs anteroposteriorly, like the sagittal, so what is lost is **lateral widening**.
- I put the ethmoid and sphenoid in the wrong order: the ethmoid is **anterior**.
