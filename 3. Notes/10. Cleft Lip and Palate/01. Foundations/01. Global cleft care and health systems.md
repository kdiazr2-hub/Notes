# Global cleft care and health systems

**In one sentence:** a cleft is a disease of **longitudinal follow-up** (birth to the end of growth), not of a single operation, so the care model that works is the one that **stays close to the family**: local, complete teams with enough volume, free care for the family and data to plan it.

|---|---|---|

**Care models**

| Model | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Surgical mission / operative camp** | Useful where there is almost no infrastructure (e.g., Haiti) | Palates neglected for lips; 86.4 % of patients not told about team care and follow-up; no local infrastructure; keeps dependence |
| **Supporting local centers** (Smile Train + SBCP "Cleft Week") | Trains local surgeons, builds infrastructure; more sustainable in Brazil, Chile, India | Needs a minimum base to build on |
| **Philanthropic center** (SOBRAPAR) | Mission: decentralize, train surgeons who return home, research; diversified income (Bazaar) | Depends on donors + SUS |
| **University center** (USP, HRAC-Bauru) | Training, research, genetics, complex syndromes, Spina's classification | Receives sequelae and late adults from the whole country |
| **Public-private partnership** (Operation Smile, Guwahati, India) | Fee-for-service from government, free for patients, audited; 250–300 cases/month, >90 % local staff, self-sufficient in 5 years | Specific to its region |

**Lancet Commission on Global Surgery: 5 pillars of a National Surgical Plan** (Alonso, Table 3.1)

1. **Infrastructure:** % of population within **2 h** of a first-level surgical facility.
2. **Workforce:** density of surgeons/anesthetists/obstetricians; training, retention, task-sharing.
3. **Service delivery:** volume, coordination, quality and safety; integrate NGOs into the national framework.
4. **Financing:** universal coverage, minimize user fees; measure **catastrophic and impoverishing expenditure**.
5. **Information management:** data systems to monitor processes, costs and outcomes.

## Essentials

1. **Late presenter with complete cleft lip and palate → palate first**, because the palate's benefit is **age-dependent** (speech is learned in the first years) and the lip's is not (stigma is removed at any age) (Alonso, pp. 5, 17). Late palate repair may not improve speech and can worsen nasal regurgitation.
2. **Care must be local**, because the child needs 15+ years of speech therapy, orthodontics, ENT, psychology and secondary surgery, and none of that can be done by migrating or by a one-week mission (pp. 4, 8).
3. **Decentralize, but only into complete centers**: minimum ACPA/Eurocleft team (plastic surgery, dentistry, psychology, speech pathology, ENT) and **80–100 new cases/year**, because "neither a non-prepared center nor a surgeon working alone should operate on a cleft patient" and their **gross sequelae** need multiple costly reoperations (pp. 8, 10).
4. **The social worker drives adherence** in Latin America, because patients drop out of follow-up, not out of surgery (p. 10).
5. **Payment shapes behavior:** a flat fee makes each extra patient a cost; **fee-for-service** makes it income and **incentivizes productivity** (Guwahati). Patients must pay nothing, because out-of-pocket costs are catastrophic for poor families (pp. 4, 18, 21).
   ![[Alonso Fig 2.3 - Brazil map of protection against impoverishing expenditure.jpg|340]]
   *Alonso, Fig. 2.3: share of the population protected against impoverishing health expenditure. The North-East (red) is the least protected, and it is also where the fewest cleft centers are.*
6. **You can't plan what you don't measure:** make cleft a **compulsory notification** (p. 4).
7. **Cleft repair is a good investment:** lifetime benefit **$145 000–390 000 per palate** and $52 000–141 000 per lip; more cost-effective than treating heart disease or HIV, or than BCG vaccination (p. 18).

## Don't confuse

| A | B | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|---|
| **Incidence** (cases per birth) | **Burden / access** (cases per surgeon) | LMICs carry 94 % of anomalies because most births and fewest surgeons are there, not necessarily a higher rate per birth |
| Access to **surgery** | Access to **follow-up** | The operation is once; follow-up lasts 15 years and must be near home |
| Mission / camp (foreign surgeons operate) | Capacity building (train and equip local teams) | Ask: when the visitors leave, who does the speech therapy and the fistula repair? |
| Flat monthly fee | Fee-for-service | Flat = more patients is a loss; per-service = more patients is income |
| Decentralization (many complete centers) | Fragmentation (isolated low-volume surgeons) | ≥80–100 new cases/year and a full team |

## Case

A rural state with no cleft center; families travel 12 h to the capital. A foreign NGO offers to come 2 weeks a year to operate 150 cases. You see a **4-year-old** girl with an unrepaired complete unilateral cleft lip and palate; her mother is a farm worker with three other children. Plan for the girl and for the state.

> [!example]- Resolution
> - **Girl:** palate first (speech is time-dependent; the lip can wait). Then local follow-up for years: speech therapy, orthodontics, ENT/audiology, psychology, lip repair, possible alveolar bone graft, VPI surgery or fistula repair. A **social worker** ensures adherence; treatment, follow-up and transport are **free**.
> - **NGO:** accept only if it **trains and equips the local team** (Cleft Week model) and does not prioritize lips over palates.
> - **System (pillars):** compulsory notification (Information); free care for the whole pathway and fee-for-service payment to the center (Financing); fellowships for local surgeons who commit to return (Workforce); **one complete center** with the full team and ≥80–100 new cases/year instead of scattered surgeons (Infrastructure / Service delivery).

## Test yourself

> [!question]- Late presenter with complete cleft lip and palate, only one operation possible: which, and why?
> The palate. Its benefit (speech) depends on age; the lip's benefit (removing stigma) does not.

> [!question]- Why do surgical missions fail in countries with growing infrastructure?
> No longitudinal follow-up (86.4 % of camp patients were not told about team care), lips prioritized over palates, no local infrastructure built, and dependence maintained.

> [!question]- Two consequences of patient migration
> For the family: the mother and child leave, the household breaks up, and the cycle of poverty continues. For the child: surgery yes, but no ancillary treatment or full rehabilitation.

> [!question]- Minimum cleft team according to ACPA/Eurocleft
> Plastic surgery, general dentistry, psychology, speech-language pathology and otolaryngology.

> [!question]- Minimum annual volume for a cleft center, according to Alonso
> 80–100 new cases per year.

> [!question]- What explains 5000 expected vs <2000 registered clefts in Brazil, and what is the fix?
> Underreporting (or overestimation of the expected rate). Fix: compulsory notification.

> [!question]- Why did fee-for-service work in Guwahati?
> Each operation brings income, so productivity is incentivized; patients pay nothing; TATA provided auditing to control the risks.

> [!question]- Name the 5 pillars of a National Surgical Plan
> Infrastructure, workforce, service delivery, financing, information management.

## My mistakes

- I thought of the problem as **access to surgery** (getting to the OR on time). The real problem is **access to follow-up**: the operation is once, the follow-up is 15 years and must be near home.
- I proposed **more missions/campaigns** to close the reporting gap, right after learning that missions don't provide follow-up and keep dependence. The gap is a **registration** problem: compulsory notification.
- I mixed **incidence** with **access**: clefts aren't necessarily more frequent in rural areas; there are fewer surgeons per case there.
- I missed the **incentive** effect of fee-for-service: I saw only the budget mismatch, not that per-service payment rewards operating more patients.
- I forgot **who pays for poor surgery**: gross sequelae requiring multiple reoperations at referral centers.
- My follow-up list (speech + orthodontics) left out **ENT/audiology, psychology and secondary surgery**, and I said "free surgery" instead of **free care for the whole pathway**.
