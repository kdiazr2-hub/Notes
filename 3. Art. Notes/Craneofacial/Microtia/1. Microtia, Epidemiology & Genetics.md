> [!abstract] Introduction, microtia
> Microtia occurs more frequently in men than women (20-40% more) and 77-93% are unilateral. 
> The right ear is affected 60% in unilateral cases. 
>

## Prevalence

Prevalence rates are from 0.8 to 4.34 per 10.000 births with higher risk for individuals of Asian heritage, Pacific Islanders and individuals of Hispanic descent.  ***In America there is a higher prevalence in Ecuador, Chile and Mexico.*** 

## Human disorders with microtia 

20-60% of people with microtia have associated anomalies or an identifiable syndrome. 

![[Pasted image 20260726201327.png|305]]

Microtia is common feature of [[Craniofacial microsomia]], [[Townes-Brocks syndrome]] and mandibular dysostosis like [[Nager syndrome]] and [[Treacher-Collins]].

## Known risk factors

* Low birth weight
* Higher maternal parity
* Maternal acute illness 
* Use of medications (retinoids, thalidomine and inmunosuppressant)
* Maternal diabetes mellitus
* Multiple births 
* Advanced maternal age
* Low maternal education
* Hispanic ethnicity

> [!tip] To bear in mind
> *Perioconceptional intake of folic-acid-containing supplements has been associated with reduce risk of microtia among non-obese women*

## Genetics of microtia 

### Animal models studies

Murine models in particular are commonly used to study developmental mechanisms involved in the formation of the head and face. 
### [[Hox genes]]

Homeobox genes are involved in the development of the pharyngeal arches.  They encode highly conserved transcription factors that control positional identity of cells and morphogenesis throughout the development. 

- Hox gene family, inactivation of Hoxa1 in mice results in hypoplastic external ears and abnormalities of the middle and inner ear. 

- Mutation of compound Hoxa1/Hoxb1 show anotia. 

- Hoxa2 knockout mice present with microtia

- Hoxb6 and Hoxa7 deficient mice present with microtia in addition to open-eyes and cleft palate.
