> [!abstract] Bottom line
> Autologous cancellous bone from the anterior iliac crest is the **gold standard** graft for [[Alveolar bone grafting|alveolar cleft repair]]. The two main harvest techniques are **open** (more bone, including cortical bone) and **trephine** (less morbidity, less and less predictable bone). Donor-site morbidity depends heavily on the **analgesic protocol**.

## Indications
- Graft material for [[Alveolar bone grafting|secondary alveolar bone grafting]] in cleft lip and palate. It is considered the gold standard because it combines osteoconduction, osteoinduction and osteogenesis ([[Alveolar cleft reconstruction utilizing a particulate autogenous tooth graft and a novel slipt technique|Molnar]]; [[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- The anterior iliac crest is the preferred donor site, as described by Boyne and Sands ([[Alveolar cleft reconstruction utilizing a particulate autogenous tooth graft and a novel slipt technique|Molnar]]).

## Harvest techniques

| | Open | Trephine |
|---|---|---|
| Cancellous volume | Higher (~2× trephine) | Lower, unpredictable; often needs supplementation |
| Cortical bone (nasal floor) | Yes (inner table) | No |
| Operative time | 2.5-3.3 h | 1.9-2.4 h |
| Ambulation / length of stay | Slower / longer | Faster / shorter (confounded by analgesia) |
| Postop opioids | More | Less |
| Reported complications | Rare | None reported |

Source for the table: pediatric systematic review of 5 comparative studies, 257 patients ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).

### Open technique
- The contemporary **minimally invasive open** approach uses a small incision, harvests from the **medial table** and limits periosteal stripping. It has substantially reduced donor morbidity compared with historical reports ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- Variants: [[Medial Trap Door Technique to Harvest a Cancellous Bone Graft From the Anterior Iliac Crest|medial trap door]] · [[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvesting, Modified Technique for Reduction of Complications|modified technique (Pokharel)]] _(not yet summarized)_

### Trephine technique
- Yields about **2 cc per pass**, up to ~6 cc with 3 passes (Sharma, cited in [[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]). Another series obtained only 0.46 cc per core, 2.3-3.2 cm³ with 5-7 passes (Caminiti, cited in Todd).
- **Keep depth ≤ 3 cm** to avoid visceral injury and fracture of the inner or outer table ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- Cannot provide a usable cortical graft; in children the crest cap is mostly cartilaginous ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).

## Age and anatomy
- Cancellous width of the crest increases from **7.21 mm at 8 years to 10.97 mm at 12 years** (CT study cited in [[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- Theoretical maximum safe volume rises from 12 cc at 8 years to 17 cc at 12 years. This CT projection has not been confirmed clinically ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- With the trend toward grafting younger children (from ~6 years), the safety of trephine harvest in a thin crest remains unknown ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).

## Perioperative analgesia
Adjuncts that reduce pain, time to ambulation and length of stay **regardless of technique** ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]):
- Local bupivacaine infiltration (subcutaneous and deep periosteal).
- Bupivacaine-soaked GelFoam® at the donor site.
- Donor-site ropivacaine infusion catheter ("pain pump"). With it, length of stay fell to **0.5 days**.
- Ultrasound-guided **TAP block**.
- IV acetaminophen.

## Complications
- Historical complications of open harvest: blood loss, dysesthesia, contour deformity, persistent pain and gait disturbance. Most of these reports are more than 20 years old ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- In comparative pediatric studies, complications were rare with both techniques. With open harvest: infection, hematoma, paresthesia and painful ambulation. With trephine: none reported ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- Donor-site reconstruction after harvest: [[A Novel Technique for Iliac Crest Reconstruction After Autologous Bone Harvest]] _(not yet summarized)_.

> [!tip] High-yield
> - ICBG is the gold standard because it is osteogenic, osteoinductive and osteoconductive.
> - Open harvest gives more bone plus cortical bone; trephine gives less pain but less bone.
> - Trephine depth ≤ 3 cm.
> - Analgesia protocol ≈ as important as the technique.
