> [!abstract] Bottom line
> In children undergoing alveolar cleft repair, **trephine** iliac crest harvest gives shorter operative time, faster ambulation, shorter stay and less opioid use than **open** harvest, but yields **less and less predictable cancellous bone** (often needing supplementation) and **no cortical bone**. Peri-operative **pain adjuncts** affect outcomes as much as the harvest technique. The authors chose a *minimally invasive short-incision open* technique plus a multimodal pain pathway.

**Full title:** *Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair: A Systematic Review Comparing Minimally Invasive Trephine and Conventional Open Techniques*. Todd AR, Fitzpatrick S, Cawthorn TR, Fraulin FOG, Harrop AR. University of Calgary / Alberta Children's Hospital. *Plast Surg* 2024;32(1):78-85. DOI: 10.1177/22925503221088840

## Study design & population
- PRISMA systematic review; MEDLINE, EMBASE and Scopus searched in January 2021.
- Inclusion: studies **directly comparing** open vs trephine [[Iliac crest bone graft|ICBG]] harvest in a pediatric population (mean age < 18) undergoing [[Alveolar bone grafting|alveolar cleft repair]].
- 422 records screened → **5 studies** (McCanny 1998, Burstein 2000, Thuaksuban 2010, Sharma 2011, Bykowski 2019).
- **257 patients**: 116 open (164 cleft sites), 141 trephine (200 cleft sites). Mean age ~11 years (range 6-30).
- **No meta-analysis**: outcome reporting was too heterogeneous, so the paper reports ranges of the study means.

## Key findings

| Outcome | Open | Trephine |
|---|---|---|
| Length of stay (days) | 1.25-5.4 | 1.0-5.0 (0.5 with pain pump) |
| Time to unassisted ambulation (h) | 20-67 | 16-46 |
| Total operative time (h) | 2.5-3.3 | 1.9-2.4 |
| Harvest time alone (min, Burstein) | 20 | 11.4 |
| Postop IV morphine (mg/kg, Sharma) | 1.64 | 0.31 |
| Pain score at discharge (/10, Sharma) | 3.1 | 0.26 |
| Duration of postop analgesia (h) | 26 | 16.5 |
| Cancellous volume (mL, Thuaksuban) | 2.53 | 1.22 |

### Bone volume
- Trephine yielded **less cancellous bone**; open harvest gave roughly double the volume (Thuaksuban).
- Supplementation was needed in **all** trephine patients in Thuaksuban (deproteinized bovine bone) and in **54%** of trephine patients in Bykowski (demineralized bone). No open-harvest patient needed supplementation.
- Sharma reported up to **6 cc with 3 passes (~2 cc per pass)** of the trephine.
- Trephine **cannot provide cortical bone** for the nasal floor. In children the cortical cap is largely cartilaginous anyway.

### Pain adjuncts: an important confounder
- Three of the five studies used no adjuncts. The others used bupivacaine-soaked GelFoam® at the donor site, local bupivacaine infiltration (subcutaneous and deep periosteal), an infraorbital nerve block, IV acetaminophen and a donor-site **ropivacaine infusion catheter**.
- Bykowski length of stay: open **2.4 d** · trephine **2.7 d** · trephine + pain pump **0.5 d** · no-harvest control **0.9 d**. The anesthetic infusion, not the trephine itself, drove the reduction in stay. Almost half of the pain-pump patients were discharged directly from PACU.
- Outside the included studies, an ultrasound-guided **TAP block** lowered pain scores and shortened time to ambulation compared with local infiltration (Shenoy).

### Complications
- **Trephine:** none reported.
- **Open:** 1 infection, 1 hematoma, 1 donor-site paresthesia and 3 cases of painful ambulation. There was also 1 case of urinary retention and 1 seizure.
- Historical complications of open harvest (blood loss, dysesthesia, contour deformity, persistent pain, gait disturbance) mostly come from reports **more than 20 years old**. Modern minimally invasive open harvest (small incision, medial table, limited periosteal stripping) has reduced donor morbidity.

### Safety of trephine in children
- **Limit trephine depth to 3 cm**: no visceral injury in 129 patients (Stranix, CT study). Depth beyond 30 mm increases the risk of fracturing the inner or outer table (Caminiti).
- Cancellous width increases from **7.21 mm at age 8 to 10.97 mm at age 12** (p < .001). Safety in younger children with a thin crest remains unknown.
- Theoretical maximum harvest volume: **12 cc at 8 years → 17 cc at 12 years** (CT projection, Stranix). This has **not been confirmed clinically**: Caminiti obtained 0.46 cc per core, 2.3-3.2 cm³ with 5-7 passes.

## Limitations
- Only 5 small, heterogeneous, non-randomized comparative studies, with no meta-analysis.
- Uneven use of analgesic adjuncts confounds the pain, ambulation and stay outcomes.
- Samples too small to detect rare serious complications such as visceral injury.
- Mean ages of 9-14 years limit applicability to younger children.
- Excluded alternative techniques: percutaneous needle, cylindrical osteotome, Volkmann curette, bone grinder, CORB needle and others.

## Clinical relevance
- There is **clinical equipoise** between the two techniques; the choice depends on the surgeon and institution.
- The authors' pathway: a **minimally invasive short-incision open technique**, harvesting a piece of **inner-table cortical bone** for nasal floor reconstruction, plus a standardized multimodal pain protocol (local and regional anesthesia, bupivacaine GelFoam® at the donor site).
- Takeaway: **pain management matters as much as harvest technique** for donor-site morbidity.

> [!tip] High-yield
> - ICBG is the **gold standard** for pediatric alveolar cleft repair.
> - Trephine means less morbidity but less bone, unpredictable volume and no cortical bone.
> - Keep trephine depth **≤ 3 cm** to avoid visceral injury and table fracture.
> - Analgesic adjuncts (donor-site infusion, TAP block, bupivacaine GelFoam®) reduce stay and pain **whatever technique is used**.

> [!question]- Repaso
> 1. What are the two most common pediatric ICBG harvest techniques? → Conventional open and minimally invasive trephine.
> 2. Main disadvantages of trephine harvest? → Lower, unpredictable cancellous volume (often needs supplementation) and no cortical bone for the nasal floor.
> 3. Why is the shorter length of stay with trephine questionable? → Confounded by analgesic adjuncts; in Bykowski, trephine alone (2.7 d) was not shorter than open (2.4 d), but trephine + pain pump was (0.5 d).
> 4. Safe maximum trephine depth in children? → 3 cm.
> 5. Which harvest technique did the authors adopt and why? → Short-incision open, to obtain enough cancellous bone plus inner-table cortical bone, combined with multimodal analgesia.
