> [!abstract] Bottom line
> Grafting of the alveolar cleft, usually in the **mixed dentition before the cleft-side canine erupts**. It restores arch continuity, supports the alar base and closes the [[Oronasal fistula|oronasal fistula]]. Autologous [[Iliac crest bone graft|iliac crest bone]] is the gold standard.

## Goals
- Proper arch support and form, support of the alar base and closure of the oronasal fistula ([[Alveolar cleft reconstruction utilizing a particulate autogenous tooth graft and a novel slipt technique|Molnar]]).
- Helps orthodontic and prosthodontic treatment, stabilizes the maxillary arch and improves oral hygiene by eliminating oronasal fistulae ([[Alveolar Bone Grafting and Fistula Closure|Junn]]).

## Timing
- Usually done in the **mixed dentition** (secondary ABG), before eruption of the cleft-side canine ([[Alveolar cleft reconstruction utilizing a particulate autogenous tooth graft and a novel slipt technique|Molnar]]; [[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- Some recommend waiting until the canine root is **1/3 to 2/3 formed** ([[Alveolar cleft reconstruction utilizing a particulate autogenous tooth graft and a novel slipt technique|Molnar]]).
- The trend is toward operating at younger ages, from ~6 years ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).

## Graft material
- **Autologous bone = gold standard** (osteoconduction, osteoinduction, osteogenesis). The preferred site is the [[Iliac crest bone graft|iliac crest]] ([[Alveolar cleft reconstruction utilizing a particulate autogenous tooth graft and a novel slipt technique|Molnar]]; [[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
- Alternatives: particulate autogenous tooth graft ([[Alveolar cleft reconstruction utilizing a particulate autogenous tooth graft and a novel slipt technique|Molnar]]). Bone substitutes, alone or mixed with autogenous bone, may achieve similar long-term bone height; this evidence is cited in [[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]].

## Surgical technique
- Flap design: the **four-flap technique** (Nordin and Abyholm) is the most common ([[Alveolar cleft reconstruction utilizing a particulate autogenous tooth graft and a novel slipt technique|Molnar]]).
- Step-by-step technique with fistula closure: see [[Alveolar Bone Grafting and Fistula Closure]].
- A small piece of cortical bone is often used to rebuild the **nasal floor** ([[Iliac Crest Bone Graft Harvest for Alveolar Cleft Repair|Todd]]).
