- Microtia has an incidence of 1 in 6,000
- It is 2:1 more common in males
- It is more frequent on the right side

The etiology is not confirmed, but there are theories that associate it with alterations in the stapedial artery and hemorrhages in the ear during embryonic development.

![[Pasted image 20260714220701.png]]

Additionally, the following medications have been reported to cause it:

1. Thalidomide
2. Retinoic acid
3. Isotretinoin
4. Clomiphene citrate

Tanzer classification, modified by Brent:

![[Pasted image 20260714220632.png]]

- **Grade I (Anotia):** Complete absence of the structures that make up the ear.

- **Grade II:** Complete hypoplasia

	- A: With atresia of the external auditory canal (EAC)

	- B: Without atresia of the EAC

- Grade III: Hypoplasia of the middle third of the ear

- Grade IV: Hypoplasia of the upper third of the auricle

	- A: Contracted ear (Cup-like and drooping)

	- Cryptotia

	- Hypoplasia of the entire upper third

	* Grade V: Prominent ear

For reconstruction, the following methods can be performed:

* Autologous rib grafts or [[Auriculoplasty, incomplete]]
* Reconstruction with a prosthesis; [[Medpord]]
* Placement of [[Removable ear prostheses]]
